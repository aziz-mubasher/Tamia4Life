Not who's free. Who understands where you're coming from.

A rules-based, transparent matcher for Phase 1 — culture and language first, every ranking explainable. The moat, specified.

What it optimizes for

Principles

The one rule: mother tongue is never traded away for availability. A sooner slot in the wrong language is not a match — it's a failure the engine must refuse to make.

Explainable by design

Every ranking produces human-readable reasons. v1 is rules-based, not learned — trust depends on the member seeing why.

Culture & language first

Language capability is a hard gate; cultural and lived-experience alignment is the heaviest soft signal. Availability breaks ties, it doesn't lead.

Fair to new facilitators

No ratings exist yet, so v1 never ranks on them. A light fairness boost gives new, vetted facilitators real exposure.

Inputs

The model

Two stages: filter, then score

First, hard filters remove anyone who cannot serve this member. Then the survivors are scored on weighted soft signals and ranked. Two stages, so the language rule can never be outweighed.

Stage 1 — Hard filters (pass / fail — no partial credit)

LanguageFacilitator speaks the member's chosen language at professional level or above. No exceptions in v1.
StatusFacilitator is active and has completed vetting.
AvailabilityHas at least one open slot inside the member's booking window.
CapacityCurrent caseload is below their maximum.

Stage 2 — Weighted score (each signal normalized 0–1, then × weight; weights sum to 100)

SignalWeightWhat earns a high score
Language quality30Native mother-tongue match scores 1.0; professional fluency scores lower. The member's language, spoken as theirs.
Cultural alignment25Shared or adjacent cultural background, same region, and lived experience of relocation.
Focus overlap20Facilitator specialties cover the member's stated focus area.
Availability fit15Has a slot soon and within preferred days/times.
Fairness boost10Lightly favors under-utilized and newer (vetted) facilitators. Internal only — never shown as a reason.

The formula

Scoring, in pseudocode

Deliberately simple and inspectable. Anyone on the team can read it, and any score can be traced back to its signals.

function rankFacilitators(member, facilitators):
  # Stage 1 — eliminate anyone who cannot serve this member
  eligible = [f for f in facilitators if
      speaksLanguage(f, member.language, minProficiency="professional")
      and f.active and f.vetted
      and hasOpenSlot(f, member.window)
      and caseload(f) < f.maxCaseload ]

  # Stage 2 — score the survivors
  for f in eligible:
    f.score = 0.30*languageQuality(f, member)     # native > professional
            + 0.25*culturalAlignment(f, member)   # region + lived experience
            + 0.20*focusOverlap(f, member)        # specialties ∩ needs
            + 0.15*availabilityFit(f, member)     # soon + preferred times
            + 0.10*fairnessBoost(f)               # exposure for new/under-used
    f.display = round(f.score * 100)
    f.reasons = topReasons(f, member, max=3)     # human-readable, in member.language

  return sortDesc(eligible, by="score")[:3]        # show top 3, tie-break on fairness

Explainability

How "why this match" is generated

Reasons are not decoration — they're the top-contributing signals, turned into a sentence in the member's own language. Show the 2–3 highest contributors above a threshold; drop anything below it; never surface the fairness boost.

Edge handling

Cold-start, ties & the no-match ladder

Cold-start & tie-breaks

  • New facilitators get a neutral baseline (no ratings used) plus the fairness boost, so they accrue sessions and become rankable on real data by Phase 2.
  • Ties break toward the higher fairness boost — spreading demand across the network.
  • Repeat exposure is dampened so the same three faces don't always surface first.

Privacy inside matching

  • The engine reads member context to rank, but the facilitator card shows only privacy-safe context.
  • Employer data is never an input or output.
  • Match reasons expose the member's own stated context back to them — nothing new is inferred and shared.

When no facilitator passes the filters — the fallback ladder

Widen availability first. Extend the booking window before touching anything about language or culture — supply, not fit, is usually the gap.

Offer a bridge language, clearly flagged. Only if the member indicated a secondary language they're comfortable in — and say so explicitly. Never silent.

Waitlist + notify. Register interest in their mother tongue and alert them the moment a match opens. The language requirement stays intact.

Meanwhile, serve value. Surface an in-language self-guided resource so the member isn't left empty-handed while supply catches up.

Build notes

Scope for v1

In v1 — build this

  • Rules-based two-stage matcher with the weights above.
  • A tunable weights config — weights live in config, not hard-coded, so ops can adjust without a deploy.
  • Reason generator wired to the localized string layer.
  • The fallback ladder and waitlist.

Deferred to v2 (Phase 2–3)

  • Learned weights tuned on real usage data.
  • Outcome- & rating-based ranking once history exists.
  • Group / masterclass matching.
  • Re-match on dissatisfaction & continuity preference (same facilitator).
