Customer Discovery Interview Kit

Learn whether the problem is real — before building the answer.

Screeners, scripts, note template and the A1–A7 scorecard. Zero interviews run so far. This is the gate everything else waits behind.

How to interview

The one rule

Never mention Tamia4Life until the interview is over. The moment you describe the idea, people start being polite, and politeness is worthless data. Ask about their life and what they have already done — not about your product.

Do not ask

  • “Would you use a service like this?” — hypothetical, always yes
  • “Do you think this is a good idea?” — asks for a compliment
  • “How much would you pay for it?” — invented number
  • “Is it hard being far from home?” — leading; you've supplied the answer
  • “Would your company buy this?” — no one can answer for procurement

Ask instead

  • “Tell me about the last time that happened.” — a specific past event
  • “What did you actually do about it?” — behaviour, not intention
  • “Who else have you talked to about it?” — reveals real weight
  • “What did that cost you — money, time, sleep?” — quantifies the pain
  • “Walk me through how the last purchase like this got approved.” — the real buying process

The signal that matters most

Does the pain surface unprompted? If you have to introduce the topic of emotional strain, language, or isolation before they mention it, that is a weak signal — record it honestly. The strongest evidence is a problem they raise themselves, and have already spent money, time or effort trying to solve.

What we are testing

The seven load-bearing assumptions

Every question in this kit maps to one of these. A2 and A4 are the sharpest knives — if either fails, the venture as designed does not work. Weight your listening toward them.

Members experience ongoing strain from adaptation, isolation or dual-life pressure — not a one-off bad week.

Script 1 of 3

End user interview

45 minutes. Conducted in their mother tongue wherever possible — Wave 1 recruiting is Arabic and Romanian speakers first, since those are the launch languages.

End users

Target: 15–25 · waves of ~5, synthesise between waves

Screener — recruit if

  • Born outside Italy, or raised in a household where Italian was not the first language
  • Currently living and working or studying in Italy
  • Arrived within the last 10 years (prioritise 1–5 years)
  • Speaks Arabic or Romanian as a mother tongue (Wave 1 focus; widen later)
  • Exclude friends and family — they will be kind, and kindness is not data

Opening — build ground, no pitch warm-up

  1. Tell me how you came to be in Italy.
  2. What does a normal week look like for you right now?
  3. What's been the hardest part of the last year? Stay silent after this. Do not suggest categories. What they name first is the finding.

Life and strain tests A1 · A7

  1. When you're carrying something heavy — stress, worry, homesickness — what do you usually do with it?
  2. Tell me about the last time you felt that way. What was happening? Get a specific, dated event. Not a general pattern.
  3. How often does that come around?
  4. Who in your life knows about it?
  5. Is there anything you'd never talk about with family back home? Probes the dual-life pressure without leading.

What they've already tried tests A3 — the strongest evidence

  1. Have you ever looked for help with any of that? Tell me what you did.
  2. What did you search for? Where did you look? Exact words matter — they reveal how people frame their own problem.
  3. What happened? Did you go through with it?
  4. If you stopped or never started — what got in the way?
  5. Have you ever paid for anything related to this? How much? Past spending is the single best predictor of future spending.

Language and culture tests A2 — sharpest knife

  1. When you talk about something personal, which language do you use?
  2. Have you ever tried to explain something personal in Italian or English? How did that go?
  3. Was there anything you couldn't say — or that came out wrong? Push gently for a concrete example. This is the heart of A2.
  4. If someone offered you support from a person who spoke your language and had lived what you've lived — how would that be different, if at all? The one near-hypothetical worth asking. Watch their face, not just the words.

Work and energy tests the productivity throughline

  1. Has any of this ever affected your work or study? Tell me about a specific time.
  2. Has your employer ever offered anything for wellbeing? Did you use it? Why or why not?

Close

  1. Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
  2. Who else should I talk to?

Listen for

The pain named before you introduce it · a specific dated event, not a generalisation · money or time already spent · an example of something they could not say in a second language · an employer benefit that went unused because it didn't fit them.

Script 2 of 3

Employer buyer interview

30–40 minutes. Target HR, People, D&I or Operations leads at employers with multicultural workforces — logistics, manufacturing, food processing, facilities, large care cooperatives. This is where the revenue is, and A4/A5 are where the venture most plausibly fails.

Employer buyers

Target: 8–12

Screener — recruit if

  • Employer has a significant share of foreign-born workers (ideally in Wave 1 language communities)
  • 50+ employees, with an actual HR or People function
  • Contact influences or owns wellbeing, welfare, D&I or retention
  • Based in Italy — prioritise the North, where ~58% of foreign residents live

Their world warm-up

  1. Tell me about your workforce — who works here, and how has that changed?
  2. What are your biggest people problems right now? Open. Do not seed "wellbeing" or "integration". What they say first is the finding.

The pain, quantified tests A4 — sharpest knife

  1. What's your turnover like? Does it differ between groups of workers?
  2. How long does someone take to become fully productive? Does that vary?
  3. What does it cost you when someone leaves in the first year? Push for a number. If they don't have one, that itself is a finding about A4.
  4. Absence, conflict, engagement scores — do any of those break down differently across your workforce?
  5. Who owns that problem internally? If nobody owns it, there is no buyer. Critical.
  6. Have you tried anything to fix it? What happened?

Budget and buying tests A5

  1. What do you currently spend on wellbeing, welfare or training? Italy's welfare aziendale regime is tax-advantaged — ask whether they use it.
  2. Walk me through how the last thing like that got bought. Who signed it off? The real buying process, from a real past purchase.
  3. When does your budget cycle run?
  4. What would make this a priority this year rather than next?
  5. What would stop it happening, even if you personally wanted it?

Privacy and trust

  1. If you offered something like this, what would you want to see back? Tests whether aggregate-only reporting is acceptable, or whether they expect individual data we will never provide.
  2. Have your workers ever declined to use a benefit? Do you know why?

Close — the commitment ask

  1. If we ran a pilot with a small group of your workers, would you want to be involved? Then ask for something real: a follow-up meeting with the budget owner, or a letter of intent. Enthusiasm without a next step is a no.

Listen for

A number attached to the pain · a named owner of the problem · a real past purchase you can trace · budget cycle timing · and above all, willingness to commit something — time, a meeting, an LOI. Warm words with no next step means A5 has failed, however friendly the conversation.

Script 3 of 3

Facilitator interview

30 minutes. Lighter pass this round — supply matters, but demand is the gate. Recruit in Wave 1 languages: Arabic and Romanian.

Facilitators

Target: 5–8

Screener — recruit if

  • Native or near-native in a Wave 1 language (Arabic or Romanian)
  • Has lived the migration or adaptation experience themselves
  • Background in coaching, counselling, social work, community work, mediation or teaching
  • Currently in Italy, or able to work with members in Italian time zones

Background tests A6

  1. Tell me about the work you do now.
  2. How did you come to work with people from your own community?
  3. What kinds of things do people bring to you?

The work itself tests A7

  1. Do people come to you before things get serious, or after? Directly tests whether the preventive middle space is real in practice.
  2. Where do you draw the line between support and clinical care?
  3. What do you do when someone needs more than you can give?

Viability tests A6

  1. How do you currently get paid for this kind of work?
  2. What would a session need to pay for it to be worth your time?
  3. How many hours a week could you realistically offer?
  4. What would make you say no to a platform like this? Invites honest objections rather than agreement.
  5. Do you know others doing similar work in your community? How many? Tests whether supply can scale beyond individuals.

Listen for

A viable rate expectation · realistic available hours · evidence that people do come before crisis (A7) · a healthy instinct about the clinical boundary · and whether there is a pool of similar people, or just this one exceptional individual.

Non-negotiable

Ethical guardrails

This is not therapy

Do not diagnose, do not probe trauma. If someone becomes distressed, stop the interview, stay with them warmly, and signpost real support. The interview is never more important than the person.

Never ask about legal status

Do not collect or record immigration or residency status under any circumstances. It is irrelevant to the research and dangerous to hold.

Mother tongue where possible

Interviewing in a second language about emotional strain distorts the very thing being studied. Use interpreters from the community where needed.

Consent is revocable

Informed, voluntary, and withdrawable at any time — including after the interview. No names in notes. No recordings without explicit permission.

Phase 1 exit

When discovery is done

Proceed to Phase 2 only when all of these hold:

  • A1–A3 validated with end users — the strain is real, language is the barrier, and they have already acted.
  • A4–A5 supported by at least a few employers with a costed pain, a budget owner, and genuine pilot interest.
  • At least one signed letter of intent from a pilot employer.
  • A6 plausible — 5–10 vetted facilitators ready in Wave 1 languages.

If demand validates but no buyer appears

Iterate the business model. Do not start building. A product with users and no payer is not a venture.

If the pain does not surface unprompted

Pause and reframe the problem. The premise itself needs rework before any design continues.
